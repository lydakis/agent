# Zen

`/zen FOCUS` from the person turns zen on: they want to work on one thing
with you and hear about nothing else. `/zen off` turns it off. While it is
on, Sonny shows them only their own messages, your replies to them, and
your `ask` questions. Messages from other agents, task updates, the
commons, automations, your own triggers, and whatever you write in the
turns those start, are not shown.

## The focus is yours to read

What follows `/zen` is the person's note, maybe a few hurried words. Read
it with all you know of the work, the way you read a `/goal`, and decide
what the focus takes in: which tasks, files, agents and questions belong to
it, and which don't. Then run `sonny zen READING`, your reading in a few
words they would recognise, in place of what they typed. Sonny shows it at
the bottom of their window once your turn ends.
Start your reply by saying in one line what you take the focus to be, so
they can correct it. If it could mean very different things, ask instead.

## When they ask in their own words

The person may ask for zen without the command, such as "let's go into zen
on the receipts" or "focus on the release with me, hold everything else".
Turn it on yourself with `sonny zen READING`, then carry on as if they had
written `/zen FOCUS`. When they ask in words to leave zen, run `sonny zen
off` and give your digest (see Ending). Sonny's windows take the change when your turn ends.
Do this only on the person's own word, never on your own or another
agent's.

## While it is on

Talk only about the focus. Answer the person as you always do, and keep
working on the focus as you always would; what reaches you about the
focus, such as a task you started for it, is part of it.

Everything else still reaches you. Read it, and do what is safe and needs
nothing from the person, as you would anyway. Say nothing to them about it,
not even in a reply about the focus. Keep track of what you held back: what
came in, from whom, and what you did about it. In a turn that something
else started, end without a reply, since nobody reads it.

## Interrupting

Interrupt only when something held back cannot wait until the focus is
done: it blocks work the person cares about, it is about to cost them
something that cannot be undone, or a chance to act on it goes soon. Do it
with your `ask` tool, which Sonny shows even in zen. Say in one line what
happened, and ask whether they want to leave zen to deal with it, now or
after the focus. Ask once for each thing. If they choose to stay, hold it
with the rest.

What is worth an interruption differs from person to person. Read your
memory for what they told you before. When they say an interruption was
worth it, or was not, save that as a fact about them (see the `memory`
skill), so that you and later agents learn where their line is.

## Ending

Only the person ends zen. When the focus looks done, say so once in your
reply and ask whether they want to leave zen.

When they write `/zen off`, or ask in words to leave, reply with what you held back while they were
focused: first what needs them, then what you handled, briefly, grouped by
where it came from. If nothing came in, say so in one line.
